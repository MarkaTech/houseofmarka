Every non-technical founder — and plenty of technical ones past their depth — faces the same trilemma: rent judgement (fractional CTO), rent capacity (agency/studio), or buy both (hire). The pitches all overclaim; here is the grid we walk clients through, incentives disclosed (we are the studio option, and we still send people to the other two when the grid says so).

## What each actually delivers

**Fractional CTO** (a day or two a week): judgement, vendor discipline, architecture calls, hiring filters, board translation. What it does not deliver: shipped software — a fractional leader with no team is a strategy subscription. Right when the constraint is *decisions*: pre-build vendor selection, [diligence](/insights/technical-due-diligence-checklist/), rescuing a mis-scoped project, designing the hiring plan. Cost: a fraction of an executive salary, cancellable, fast to start. Failure mode: seniority theatre — slides accumulate, nothing merges.

**Studio/agency**: shipped product from a senior team that has done this shape before — with the process ([release trains](/insights/weekly-release-trains-small-teams/), [test discipline](/insights/why-your-ai-needs-a-test-suite/), handover) arriving pre-built. What it does not deliver: institutional memory that stays when the contract ends — *unless the engagement is designed for handover*, which is the single question that separates good studios from body shops. Ask how the last three handovers went; ask what the client's team could do alone on day one after. Right when the constraint is *capacity with judgement*: the v1, the [modernization](/insights/rewrite-the-client-keep-the-core/), the [integration layer](/marketplaces/) nobody in-house has built before. Failure mode: dependency by design — recognisable by proprietary platforms, absent documentation and hostility to your future hires.

**First engineering hire(s)**: ownership, context accumulation, the beginning of a real capability. What it does not deliver, alone: senior architecture judgement (a first hire designing systems solo learns on your production), delivery process (that gets invented, slowly), or resilience ([bus factor: one](/insights/technical-due-diligence-checklist/)). Right when: recurring product work exists beyond a launch, and someone senior — fractional or studio — is shaping what they build. Failure mode: hiring a mid-level generalist into an architecture role because the market priced seniors honestly.

## The hybrid that keeps winning

The pattern that recurs across our healthiest clients: **studio builds v1 with handover designed in → fractional-or-studio architecture retainer holds the line → first hires land into a working codebase with process already running → studio recedes to the specialist edges** ([AI systems](/services/#ai), integrations, the occasional rescue). Each element covers the others' failure mode; total cost tracks below a premature in-house team and *far* below a failed one.

## The questions that settle it fast

Is the constraint decisions, capacity, or continuity? Is there recurring work after launch, or a build-then-operate shape? Can you attract a genuinely senior hire *now*, honestly? What must survive the engagement — code, docs, process, people? Write the answers down and the grid usually fills itself. When it does not, [that conversation](/contact/) is thirty minutes and we will tell you which of the three we are not.
