Ask any operations team what to automate and you get either everything ("we're drowning") or the pet grievance (the report someone hates compiling). Both answers waste the first, most credibility-rich automation budget. The audit that works is unglamorous: enumerate, score, rank, and let the arithmetic argue. We run it in two weeks; here is the method.

## Enumerate honestly

Workshop per team, one question: *what do you do repeatedly?* Capture frequency, time per instance, who does it, systems touched, and what happens when it goes wrong. The list always surprises: the [Monday copy-paste rituals](/insights/when-to-kill-the-spreadsheet/), the approval chains where the approver rubber-stamps 97%, the [return-reason coding](/insights/returns-are-a-product-problem/) nobody reads, the supplier emails re-keyed into the ERP. Fifty to ninety processes is typical for a mid-size commerce operation. Most will not survive scoring — that is the point.

## Score on four axes

- **Volume × time** — the gross prize, in hours. High-frequency-short beats low-frequency-long at equal totals: less variance, faster learning loops.
- **Specifiability** — can a competent colleague execute from written rules alone? [Fully specifiable → deterministic automation](/insights/agentic-automation-vs-rpa/); mostly-specifiable-with-judgement → agent with [approval gates](/insights/ai-agent-guardrails-production/); tacit-expertise → leave it, for now.
- **Error cost, both directions** — what a mistake costs (caps autonomy, adds review) *and* what current human error already costs (the case for change; audits regularly find the manual process erring more than anyone admitted).
- **System accessibility** — API-reachable systems score up; portal-only and [PDF-shaped inputs](/insights/ai-catalog-enrichment-scale/) score down but are increasingly model-tractable; the licence that forbids automation scores zero and goes on the renegotiation list.

Multiply, rank, and take the top six — not sixteen; program credibility is built by finishing.

## The portfolio shape that pays

The ranked list almost always yields: two **deterministic integrations** wearing automation costumes (build the [boring pipeline](/insights/integration-errors-design-for-failure/), no AI required — fastest payback in the set), two **agentic workflows** (document intake, [reconciliation](/insights/channel-pnl-marketplace-profit/), triage — the judgement-shaped middle), one **assist-not-replace** (drafting for human send, the highest-acceptance pattern in customer-facing work), and one **decline** — the pet grievance, scored honestly at last, returned with numbers and condolences.

## Instrument before victory laps

Baseline before build: hours, error rate, cycle time, unit cost. Report against baseline monthly; feed exceptions back as [eval cases](/insights/why-your-ai-needs-a-test-suite/); expand autonomy only when the log earns it. Automation programs die of unverifiable claims and unowned failures — the audit's real deliverable is a *measurement habit*, which outlives any single bot.
