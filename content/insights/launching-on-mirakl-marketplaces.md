While everyone watches Amazon, a quieter marketplace economy grew underneath: hundreds of major retailers — Carrefour, Decathlon, Leroy Merlin, Kroger, Macy's, MediaMarkt among them — run third-party marketplaces on platforms like Mirakl. For brands, these are distribution into trusted retail brands without the knife-fight competition density of the big generalists.

## Why operator marketplaces are different

On Amazon you are one of millions; on a retailer's Mirakl marketplace you are one of hundreds or thousands of *vetted* sellers, curated to extend that retailer's range. Category competition is thinner, buyers arrive with retailer-level trust, and commission structures are negotiated more often than posted. The trade: each operator runs its own onboarding, its own catalogue standards, its own service-level expectations — and they enforce them, because their brand is on your delivery.

## The onboarding reality

Expect per-operator: commercial approval (they check you fit the range), catalogue mapping to their category tree and attribute model, EAN/GTIN discipline, and quality gates on images and content. The operators' attribute requirements are stricter than Amazon's in most categories — these are retailers with merchandising standards, not open platforms.

Done manually, each marketplace is weeks of spreadsheet work, and the tenth one is as slow as the first. Done properly, your product data lives in one normalised model and each new marketplace is a *mapping* — which is precisely the [integration architecture](/marketplaces/) we build. Brands running this way launch new Mirakl operators in days.

## Operations: their SLAs, your metrics

Mirakl surfaces seller quality scores to operators: acceptance rate, dispatch time, incident rate. Fall below thresholds and operators suspend first, discuss later — their shopper promised a Carrefour-grade experience. Wire orders into the same consolidated pipeline as your other channels, with the same stock truth, and the metrics take care of themselves. Run it as a side spreadsheet and the suspension email is a matter of time.

## Where this fits in a channel strategy

Operator marketplaces shine for brands with established catalogue quality expanding into new European markets — the retailer's trust substitutes for the brand awareness you have not built yet. B2B versions (Mirakl powers many) open wholesale channels with the same machinery. Margins often beat the generalist marketplaces once you net out advertising, because you are not paying to be findable in an infinite aisle.

The pattern we recommend: pick two operators adjacent to your category strength, onboard through your normalised catalogue (not their spreadsheets), prove the ops metrics, then scale the mapping to the next ten.
